Quick Glossary

TermMeaning
DragUnnatural movement of a floating fly caused by the line. Trout often refuse dragging flies.
EmergerInsect in the process of leaving the nymphal/pupal stage and reaching the surface.
IndicatorStrike indicator (yarn, foam, etc.) used to detect nymph takes.
MendRepositioning the line on the water to improve the drift of the fly.
PresentationHow the fly is delivered and drifts — the most important part of fly fishing.
TippetThe thin end section of the leader that attaches to the fly.
X ratingLeader/tippet diameter system (e.g. 4X, 5X). Higher number = thinner.
Dead driftA fly floating or sinking at the speed of the current with no artificial pull.
SwingLetting a fly or streamer arc across the current on a tight line.
ReddSpawning bed dug by trout in gravel. Avoid wading on them.

Tippet Size Guide (approx.)

XDiameterTypical use
3X~0.008"Larger flies, streamers
4X~0.007"General dry & nymph
5X~0.006"Most trout dry flies
6X~0.005"Small flies, clear water
7X~0.004"Very selective fish

Suggested Starter Kit

  • 9' 5-wt rod + matching reel & floating line
  • 9' leaders (4X & 5X) + tippet spools
  • Box with: Woolly Buggers, Pheasant Tails, Hare’s Ears, Adams, Elk Hair Caddis
  • Nippers, forceps, strike indicators, split shot
  • Polarized sunglasses (essential for seeing fish and structure)

Further Learning (Free & Low-Cost)

  • Local fly shops — clinics, river reports, and friendly advice
  • State wildlife / fisheries websites — regulations, stocking, access
  • Public-domain and classic texts (many older fly-fishing books are free online)
  • Practice casting on grass; film yourself to check form
Remember: Time on the water beats any book or website. Start simple, observe, and adjust.
