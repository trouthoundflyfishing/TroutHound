Proven Trout Patterns

Classic flies that consistently catch trout. Materials and tying notes for each.

🪰

Woolly Bugger

Streamer
Sizes 6–12 Black / Olive / Brown Beginner

The most versatile streamer in the box. Fish it stripped, swung, or dead-drifted. Imitates leeches, baitfish, and large nymphs.

Materials Hook: Streamer 3XL · Thread: Black or olive 6/0 · Tail: Marabou · Body: Chenille · Hackle: Soft hackle palmered · Optional: Bead head or weight

Tying notes: Keep the marabou tail about shank length. Palmer the hackle sparsely. A bit of flash in the tail is optional but effective.

🪶

Pheasant Tail Nymph

Nymph
Sizes 12–18 Natural / Flashback Beginner

Frank Sawyer’s classic. Suggests mayfly nymphs and gets eaten in almost every trout stream.

Materials Hook: Nymph 2XL · Thread: Brown · Tail & body: Pheasant tail fibers · Rib: Fine copper wire · Thorax: Peacock herl · Legs: Pheasant fibers · Optional: Bead, flashback

Tying notes: Rib tightly to protect the herl. Keep the thorax a bit fuller than the abdomen. Legs should be short.

🐰

Hare’s Ear Nymph

Nymph
Sizes 10–16 Natural / Gold bead Beginner

Buggy and impressionistic. Catches fish when you need a general mayfly or caddis nymph look.

Materials Hook: Nymph · Thread: Brown · Tail: Guard hairs · Body: Hare’s ear dubbing · Rib: Gold or copper wire · Wing case: Turkey or synthetic · Legs: Picked-out dubbing · Optional: Bead

Tying notes: Don’t over-dub. Pick out the thorax for legs. A gold bead adds weight and flash.

🪰

Adams

Dry Fly
Sizes 12–18 Gray Intermediate

The classic general-purpose dry. Works as a mayfly adult or a searching pattern when fish are looking up.

Materials Hook: Dry fly · Thread: Gray · Tail: Mixed grizzly & brown hackle fibers · Body: Gray dubbing · Wing: Grizzly hackle tips · Hackle: Mixed grizzly & brown

Tying notes: Wings upright and divided. Hackle size matched to hook. Keep the body slender.

🦌

Elk Hair Caddis

Dry Fly
Sizes 12–18 Tan / Olive / Brown Intermediate

Al Troth’s high-floating caddis. Perfect for riffles and broken water where a bushy dry stays visible.

Materials Hook: Dry fly · Thread: Tan or brown · Body: Dubbing to match · Hackle: Brown or ginger, palmered · Wing: Elk or deer hair · Optional: Wire rib

Tying notes: Stack the hair, flare it slightly, and trim the butts into a clean head. Palmer the hackle before the wing.

🟠

Soft Hackle (Partridge & Orange)

Wet / Emerger
Sizes 12–16 Orange / Yellow / Green Beginner

Simple, deadly, and quick to tie. Swing or dead-drift just under the surface during hatches.

Materials Hook: Wet or standard · Thread: Orange · Body: Floss or fine dubbing · Hackle: Partridge or hen soft hackle (1–2 turns)

Tying notes: Sparse is better. One or two turns of hackle is enough. The fly should look delicate in the water.

Building a Starter Box

Carry a few of each of the patterns above in sizes 12–16. Add a couple of larger streamers (size 8–10) and a few smaller dries (18) for selective fish. That combination covers the majority of trout situations you’ll meet.

Confidence flies: When in doubt, fish a Pheasant Tail or Hare’s Ear under an indicator or as a dropper, and keep a Woolly Bugger ready for when you want to cover water fast.
